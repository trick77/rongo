# Routing, measured — and it does not yet beat not asking at all

The spec made one number mandatory before phase 4b could be written in: *"in Phase 4
wird die Trefferquote des Routens auf einem festen Fragenkatalog gegen Pro gemessen,
bevor non-Pro dort festgeschrieben wird. Behaupten reicht hier nicht."* This is that
measurement. It answers the question that was asked, and it also answers one nobody
thought to ask, whose answer is worse.

Same corpus, same catalogue, `text-embedding-3-small` at 1536 dimensions,
`/tmp/rongo-eval-small.db`, rebuilt from scratch because phase 4b squashed the schema.

## The corpus is not byte-identical to the published one

peeq 526 files / 5694 chunks, loom 530 / 3855, go-sqlite3 292 / 2171. The earlier
documents measured 523 / 5685, 530 / 3855, 292 / 2186 — the local repositories moved on
between then and now. The embedding cache did not survive either: `/tmp` had been
cleared, so the corpus was re-embedded rather than restored.

That makes the anchor arm load-bearing rather than ceremonial, and it holds:

| anchor: the original 28, first candidate only | measured | published |
|---|---|---|
| recall@5 | **0.679** | 0.679 |
| recall@20 | **0.786** | 0.786 |
| MRR | **0.476** | 0.476 |

Identical to three decimals on a corpus that drifted underneath it. Every number below
is therefore comparable with phases 2, 3 and 4a. The 44-question `unique` cohort
reproduces its control value too: recall@20 = 0.750, exactly as in the gathered-set
document.

## What was asked: does the cheap model route as well as the expensive one?

Yes — to within one question out of sixty-one.

| judge deployment | overall | `ambiguous` (want ask) | `unique`+`composition` (want no ask) |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-Pro (`ShortGate`) | 0.656 (40/61) | 0.583 (7/12) | 0.673 (33/49) |
| Pro | 0.672 (41/61) | 0.667 (8/12) | 0.673 (33/49) |

Pro finds one more genuinely ambiguous question and is otherwise identical — the same 33
of 49 correct on the questions that should not produce a clarification, the same
`composition` failure on the same question. **One question is not a reason to move the
routing judgement onto the expensive deployment**, and the spec's condition for writing
non-Pro in is met. That is the mandated finding.

It is also the least interesting thing on this page.

## What nobody asked: is asking better than not asking?

Not yet. A router that never asks anything scores **0.803 (49/61)** on this catalogue,
because 49 of the 61 questions want no clarification. Every configuration measured here
is worse than that:

| `BACKEND_ROUTE_MARGIN` | accuracy | correct/total |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | **0.754** | 46/61 |
| 0.15 | 0.705 | 43/61 |
| 0.20 | 0.689 | 42/61 |
| 0.25 | 0.672 | 41/61 |
| 0.30 | 0.672 | 41/61 |
| 0.40 | 0.689 | 42/61 |
| *never ask* | *0.803* | *49/61* |

The shape is almost monotonic: the less the router asks, the better it scores, and the
best score available is the one where it stops asking altogether. At the default margin
of 0.25 the ladder spends **16 wrong clarifications to buy 7 right ones**.

This is not a bug in the ladder's implementation. Every rung does what it was built to
do: the margin gate fires, the manifest check suppresses the composition cases without a
model call, and the judge decides the rest. The problem is upstream of all of it —
**the candidates are wrong more often than the decision about them is.**

## Where it actually goes wrong

The `unique` cohort is definitionally unambiguous: one repository, one file, one answer.
The router nevertheless asked about **8 of 44** of them. Those are not judgement errors
about ambiguity; they are cases where two modules scored close enough that the gate
opened, and the judge — looking at two plausible-looking code excerpts from two
repositories that genuinely both contain a `store` or an `httpapi` package — reasonably
said "ask".

The grounding arm shows what that costs:

| | |
|---|---|
| Sources contain the expected file, over the 36 unique questions the router did **not** ask about | **0.944** (34/36) |
| The same, over all 44 | 0.773 (34/44) |

The first number is the one comparable to the published **0.955**: one question apart,
i.e. routing does not narrow what the answer is built from, exactly as the design
required. The second number is what a reader would actually experience — a fifth of the
unambiguous questions never reach an answer at all on the first turn, because they get a
question back instead.

## The composition cohort is unchanged, as predicted

0 of 5 — no `composition` question has all its parts in the top 20, and only 1 of 10
parts is retrieved at all. `repo_deps` does its job: it stops the router from asking
about them, which is why they contribute 5 correct "no ask" decisions. It does not make
them answerable. The seam is `sql.Open("sqlite3", …)` plus a blank import — a *string*,
not a symbol — and the reference walk does not cross strings. This was stated in the
phase 4b design before the code was written and is confirmed here.

## What this means for the margin

**Do not take 0.10 from the table.** It scores best only because it asks least, and the
metric rewards silence on a catalogue that is 80 % "do not ask". Choosing the margin by
this table optimises the router towards switching itself off.

The default stays at **0.25** pending a fix to the candidate layer, and the reason is
recorded here rather than in a commit message: at 0.25 the router asks about 20 of 61
questions and gets 7 of the 12 real ones right. At 0.10 it would ask about far fewer and
find fewer of them. Neither is good; the difference between them is not where the
problem lives.

## What would move the number

The failure is in what the candidates *are*, not in how they are judged, so the two
obvious levers are:

- **Module-level candidates are too coarse.** `peeq/backend/internal/store` and
  `loom/backend/internal/store` look alike from the outside and their top chunks look
  alike too. The card asks the reader to choose between two things that are, at the
  level shown, the same thing. Phase 3 measured that module-level *ranking* helps
  nothing; this measurement suggests module-level *identity* is also the wrong grain for
  a clarification.
- **The judge sees excerpts, not a comparison.** It gets each candidate's top chunks and
  is asked whether they are alternatives. It is never shown what would distinguish them —
  which repository the question's vocabulary belongs to, which one the expanded search
  terms actually hit. `Understanding.Repos` is already computed and is not in the prompt.

Both are phase-4c questions. Neither is a reason to hold phase 4b: the round trip,
the record, the resumption from stored hits and the re-explaining all work and are
tested. What this document establishes is that the *policy* driving it is not yet
earning its keep, and the number to beat is 0.803 — a router that says nothing.
